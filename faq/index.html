The screenshots use a sample company, Acme Logistics Sdn Bhd, with made-up staff. Your screens show your own organisation's name and data. What you can see depends on your role and on the modules in your organisation's plan.

02 · Signing in and finding your way

Signing in

Your administrator gives you the web address for your organisation's HRMSv2, plus a username and password. Each organisation has its own address and its own separate data.

  1. Open your organisation's HRMSv2 address in a browser such as Chrome, Edge, Safari or Firefox.
  2. Type your Username and Password, then select Sign in.
  3. If your organisation uses single sign-on, you'll also see a button such as Sign in with SSO. Select it to sign in with your work account instead.

Forgotten your password? There's no self-service reset. Ask your HR or system administrator to set a new one for you. They can do it from Users.

Using single sign-on? Your HRMSv2 account must already exist with the same email address as your work account. If it doesn't, the sign-in page says no account uses that email. Ask your administrator to create it.

The main screen

After signing in you land on the Dashboard. Every page has the same layout:

  1. 1Sidebar menu. One entry per module. Select a module to open its list of pages; the page you're on is highlighted in orange.
  2. 2Page title. Tells you where you are.
  3. 3Notifications. The bell shows a red count when something needs your attention. Select it to open Notifications.
  4. 4Your name and role. Select it to see your username and email, or to Sign out.
  5. 5Summary tiles. Live counts such as active employees, open vacancies and approvals waiting.
  6. 6Charts. Headcount by department, the hiring funnel, performance and learning compliance.

Lists, searches and forms

Most pages are a list of records with the same controls:

  • Search box and filters above the list, for example All Statuses, narrow what you see.
  • The orange button at the top right creates something new: New Request, New Claim, Add Employee and so on. It opens a form.
  • The pencil and bin icons at the end of a row edit or delete that record, if your role allows it.
  • Fields marked * in a form are required.
  • Coloured labels show each record's status, for example APPROVED or SUBMITTED.

04 · Your plan

Modules and seats

Your organisation subscribes to HRMSv2 on a plan from Visionaries Ent. The plan decides two things.

Which modules you have

Modules that aren't in your plan don't appear in anyone's menu, whatever their role. Dashboard, Workflow, Notifications, Reports, Documents, Users and Audit are part of every plan.

How many people can sign in

Your plan may include a number of seats. Each active staff account (user type Internal) uses one seat. Applicant accounts and deactivated accounts don't count.

When every seat is in use, adding another staff user fails, and the system shows Failed to create user. Nobody who already has an account is affected.

05 · How approvals work

Maker, checker, approved

Important changes go through an approval chain before they take effect: new positions, requisitions, job postings, onboarding cases, goal plans, performance outcomes, attendance corrections and more.

The person who creates the change is the maker. One or more checkers then approve it, one step at a time. The system never lets you approve something you submitted yourself.

Each request moves through these statuses:

DRAFT→ SUBMITTED→ IN REVIEW→ APPROVED or RETURNED→RESUBMITTED or REJECTED

Every step has a target turnaround time, and the expanded request shows when each step is due.

Approving a request

HR CheckerHR AdminApprovers
  1. Open Workflow › Requests, or select the notification that told you about it.
  2. Select the row to expand it. You'll see the approval steps, the history and any comments.
  3. Choose Approve, Return or Reject.
    • Approve moves it to the next step, or completes it if yours is the last step.
    • Return sends it back to the maker. Write what needs changing (at least 5 characters).
    • Reject ends it. Give the reason.

Each step is assigned to a role. If you can't act on a request, it's probably waiting at a step for a different role, or you're the person who submitted it.

06 · For employees

Everyday self-service

EmployeeEveryone

As an employee your menu is short: Dashboard, Workforce, Performance, Learning, Workflow, Notifications, ESS, Service Desk and Claims, depending on your organisation's plan.

Applying for leave

  1. Go to Workforce › Leave Requests and select New Request.
  2. Choose the Leave Type, for example Annual Leave or Medical Leave.
  3. Enter the Start Date, End Date and number of Days. Add a Reason if you like.
  4. Select Submit Request. It shows as PENDING until it's decided, then APPROVED or REJECTED.

Claiming expenses

  1. Go to Claims › Claim Requests and select New Claim.
  2. Choose yourself as the Employee, and give the claim a Claim Title, for example "Client meeting — Petaling Jaya".
  3. Enter the Period Start and Period End the expenses cover, check the Currency, and add Notes.
  4. Select Create Claim. Follow its status in the list: SUBMITTED → IN REVIEW → APPROVED → PAID.

Self-service requests

Use ESS (employee self-service) to ask HR for something that isn't leave or a claim.

  1. Go to ESS › My Requests and select New Request.
  2. Pick a Request Type: profile update, document request (for example an employment letter), leave, claim, training, IT access, or other.
  3. Write a Subject and a Description, then select Submit Request.

To correct your personal details, such as your phone number or address, use ESS › Profile Changes. HR reviews the change before it's saved to your record.

Getting help: service desk tickets

Have a question about your payslip, leave balance or system access? Raise a ticket and the HR service desk picks it up.

  1. Go to Service Desk › Tickets and select New Ticket.
  2. Choose a Category, write a Subject, set the Priority and describe the problem.
  3. Select Submit Ticket. You can follow who it's assigned to and its status in the list.

Check Service Desk › Knowledge Base first: your answer may already be there.

Goals, reviews and learning

  • Performance › Goal Plans shows your goals for the current cycle. Select Show goals to see each goal and its weight.
  • Performance › Reviews is where you complete your self-review and see your manager's rating.
  • Learning › Assignments lists courses assigned to you, including mandatory ones such as data protection training.

08 · For HR teams

Running HR day to day

HR AdminHR MakerHR CheckerRecruiterSpecialists

Core HR: your organisation's structure

Everything else builds on Core HR, so set it up in this order:

  1. Org Units: the company, divisions and departments, each with a parent.
  2. Cost Centres: where people's costs are charged.
  3. Job Grades: levels with their pay band minimum and maximum.
  4. Jobs: the kinds of work, for example "Software Engineer".
  5. Positions: individual seats in a department. A position has a job, a grade and an org unit, and is VACANT or OCCUPIED. Mark it Critical if it needs a succession plan.
  6. Employees: people, each filling a position.

Adding an employee

  1. Go to Core HR › Employees and select Add Employee.
  2. Step 1, Personal Info: full legal name, work email, phone, date of birth, gender and nationality. Select Save & Continue.
  3. Step 2, Employment Details: employee number, hire date, employment status, position, org unit, grade and manager.

Each employee gets a number such as EMP-000004 and a status: ACTIVE, PROBATION or TERMINATED. Adding an employee doesn't create a login: an administrator does that separately in Users.

Recruitment: from vacancy to hire

Hiring follows one pipeline across the Recruitment and Onboarding modules:

  1. Requisition. An HR Maker raises one in Recruitment › Requisitions for a position, with the reason (for example backfill), headcount and target start date. An HR Checker approves it.
  2. Job posting. The recruiter creates a posting from the approved requisition and chooses whether it's External (shown on the careers portal) or internal, with opening and closing dates.
  3. Applications. Candidates apply through the careers portal. Each application has a score and a stage: SCREENING, INTERVIEW, OFFER and so on.
  4. Interviews. Use Schedule Interview to set the round, type (for example video), time and meeting link. Interviewers record feedback afterwards.
  5. Offer. Create the offer letter with basic salary, total package, employment type and start date. Finance can review it before it goes out, and the candidate's acceptance is recorded on it.
  6. Onboarding. An onboarding case opens with a checklist. Each task is marked complete and verified.

Leave, overtime and attendance

  • Workforce › Leave Types: set up each type of leave with its days per year, whether it's paid and whether unused days carry forward.
  • Workforce › Leave Requests and Workforce › Overtime: everyone's requests. HR can create a request for any employee.
  • Workforce › Transfers: moves between positions or departments.
  • Attendance › Records: check-ins and check-outs from the attendance kiosk, filtered by date and method.

Performance

  1. HR Performance creates a cycle in Performance › Cycles, for example "FY2026 Annual Performance Review", with its goal-setting and year-end review windows.
  2. Employees agree their goal plans with their manager. Goal plans go through approval.
  3. At year end, employees complete a self-review and managers a manager review, each with a rating.
  4. HR confirms the final outcomes in Performance › Outcomes, which also go through approval.

Other HR modules

LearningCourses with type, duration and pass score; assignments, completions and skill gaps. New courses are published through approval.
SuccessionSuccession plans for critical positions, talent pools and talent profiles. Used by the Talent Committee.
SkillsA skill inventory by category, and the skills recorded for each employee.
Employee RelationsGrievance and disciplinary cases with parties, evidence, hearings, outcomes and appeals.
DocumentsEmployee document records and company policies, with versions, effective dates and acknowledgement tracking.
OffboardingLeaving cases with the last working date and an exit checklist.
EngagementEmployee surveys, which can be anonymous, and recognition nominations.
HSEHealth, safety and environment incidents, including near misses, plus wellbeing programmes.
Service DeskThe HR ticket queue and the knowledge base staff search before raising a ticket.

09 · Payroll and compensation

Paying people

Payroll OfficerHR AdminFinance Checker

Set up once: pay calendars (for example a monthly payroll) in Payroll › Calendars, and elements (basic pay, allowances, deductions, each with its general-ledger account) in Payroll › Elements.

Running payroll each period

DRAFT→ PROCESSING→ LOCKED→ APPROVED→ PAID
  1. In Payroll › Payroll Runs, select New Run. Choose the Pay Calendar, the period start and end, and the pay date.
  2. Add the period's amounts in Payroll › Payslip Lines. To save time, copy the lines from the previous run and adjust them.
  3. Select Lock so the figures can't change, then Generate Payslips.
  4. A checker selects Approve, and on the pay date the run is marked paid with Mark Paid.
  5. Review the general-ledger journal in Payroll › GL Postings. If your administrator has connected your accounting system, send it from there.

Compensation, benefits and claims

  • Compensation › Salaries: everyone's current salary package by department and grade, with the total for the page.
  • Compensation › Packages and Compensation › Bonus Cycles: salary changes and bonuses, made by an HR Maker and approved by an HR Checker.
  • Benefits › Benefit Plans and Benefits › Enrollments: medical, insurance and other plans with employee and employer contribution rates.
  • Claims › Claim Requests: everyone's expense claims, to review, approve and mark paid.

Users and roles

  1. Go to Users and select Add User.
  2. Enter the first and last name, a Username and Email.
  3. Choose the Primary Role and the User Type: Internal for staff.
  4. Tick any Additional Roles. The primary role is always included.
  5. Set a starting Password and select Create User. Pass the username and password to the person securely.

To reset someone's password, open their user with the pencil icon and enter a new one. To remove access when someone leaves, set the account to inactive rather than deleting it: their history stays, and their seat is freed.

System settings

Settings (System Admin only) holds three things:

  • Currency: the currency shown everywhere for money.
  • Payroll GL posting accounts: the general-ledger accounts credited for net pay and for employer contributions.
  • Accounting system integration: the address and key of your accounting system's API, so GL postings can be sent to it directly. Until it's set, you can still export postings.

Approval rules

Workflow › Rules lists every approval chain: which kind of record it covers, how many steps it has, and which role approves at each step. The shield icon shows the maker-checker rule is on, so makers can't approve their own requests. Select a rule to see its steps.

Audit trail and reports

Audit records every sign-in, change, approval and rejection with who did it, when and from which IP address. Records can't be edited or deleted. Filter by action, module and date.

Reports includes the HR dashboard, headcount by department, the hiring funnel, performance and learning reports.

ApplicantsRecruiters

12 · Attendance kiosk

Checking in with a face scan

HREveryone who checks in

The kiosk turns any tablet or computer with a camera into a check-in point. Staff look at the camera: the first scan of the day checks them in and the next checks them out.

  1. Enrol faces. HR captures each employee's face once in Attendance › Face Enrolment.
  2. Set up the kiosk. On the device by the entrance, open your HRMSv2 address followed by /kiosk and allow camera access. The kiosk page doesn't need anyone signed in.
  3. Check in and out. The kiosk shows the person's name and whether they checked in or out. Records appear in Attendance › Records.

13 · Questions and fixes

When something doesn't look right

I can't see a module or page a colleague can see.

Either your roles are different, or the module isn't in your organisation's plan. Ask your HR Admin to check your roles in Users. If nobody in your organisation can see it, it isn't in your plan.

A list is empty, but I know there are records.

Check the filters above the list: a status filter such as Approved hides everything else. Set it back to All Statuses and clear the search box.

I forgot my password.

Ask your HR or system administrator. They set a new password for you in Users. Change it to something only you know as soon as you can, by asking them again if needed.

Why can't I approve my own request?

Maker-checker: the person who submits a change can never approve it. Someone with the approving role does. See How approvals work.

My request came back as RETURNED. What now?

Open it in Workflow › Requests and read the approver's comment. Make the changes, then submit again. It continues as RESUBMITTED.

Adding a user fails with "Failed to create user".

Check that the username and email aren't already in use. If they aren't, your organisation has probably used all the seats in its plan. Deactivate accounts for people who have left, or contact Visionaries Ent. about a larger plan.

I'm asked to sign in again.

For security, sessions end after a period without use. Sign in again; nothing you saved is lost.

Single sign-on says there's no account for my email.

Single sign-on only works for accounts that already exist. Ask your administrator to create your user with exactly the same email address as your work account.

The kiosk says "Camera unavailable".

Allow camera access for the page in the browser's site settings, then reload. The kiosk also needs to be opened over a secure (https) address.

Who do I contact for help?

For questions about your own records, raise a ticket in Service Desk. Administrators can contact Visionaries Ent. about access, plans, seats or problems with the system itself.

14 · Glossary

Terms used in HRMSv2

Maker
The person who creates or changes a record and submits it for approval.
Checker
The person who approves, returns or rejects what a maker submitted.
Workflow request
One item going through an approval chain, with its steps, history and comments.
Approval rule
The definition of an approval chain: which record it covers and which role approves at each step.
Org unit
A part of the organisation: the company, a division or a department.
Job
A kind of work, such as "Software Engineer". Many positions can share one job.
Position
One specific seat in a department, vacant or filled by an employee.
Job grade
A level with a pay band (minimum and maximum pay).
Cost centre
Where a person's costs are charged in the accounts.
Requisition
An approved request to hire for a position.
ESS
Employee self-service: requests staff send to HR themselves.
Payroll run
One pay period's payroll, from draft to paid.
Payroll element
One kind of pay or deduction, such as basic pay or an allowance.
GL posting
The accounting journal entries produced from a payroll run.
Seat
One active staff login. Your plan may limit how many you can have.
Module
One area of the system, such as Payroll or Recruitment. Your plan decides which you have.
